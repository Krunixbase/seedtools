# **Sustainability Plan — SeedTools Suite**

SeedTools Suite is designed as a **long‑term public good**, not a one‑off software release.  
Its sustainability model is built on **modularity**, **reproducibility**, **community contribution**, and **low‑maintenance deterministic architecture**.

The goal is to ensure that SeedTools remains **usable, secure, and maintained for years**, even with minimal ongoing funding.

---

## **1. Architectural Sustainability**

SeedTools is intentionally designed to be:

### **Deterministic**
- predictable outputs  
- reproducible builds  
- minimal maintenance overhead  

### **Modular**
Each module (Recovery, Forensics, Entropy, Mnemonic, Path, Scanner, Desktop, CLI, Launcher) is isolated and can be maintained independently.

### **Offline‑First**
No servers, no cloud, no backend infrastructure → **near‑zero hosting cost**.

### **Transparent & Documented**
Clear architecture, product docs, and reproducible workflows make it easy for contributors to join.

👉 **Product Architecture**

---

## **2. Long‑Term Maintenance Strategy**

SeedTools sustainability is based on four pillars:

### **1. Open‑Source Community Contributions**
- modular codebase encourages PRs  
- deterministic tests make contributions safe  
- clear documentation lowers entry barriers  

### **2. NGO Partnerships**
NGOs benefit directly from SeedTools and often support:

- field testing  
- UX improvements  
- documentation  
- safety workflows  

### **3. Periodic Grants (not continuous funding)**
SeedTools does **not** require constant funding.  
Instead, it uses:

- mini‑grants for small improvements  
- annual grants for major releases  
- targeted grants for security audits  

### **4. Low‑Cost Infrastructure**
Because SeedTools is offline‑first:

- no servers  
- no cloud costs  
- no telemetry infrastructure  
- no backend maintenance  

This makes long‑term sustainability **extremely cost‑efficient**.
